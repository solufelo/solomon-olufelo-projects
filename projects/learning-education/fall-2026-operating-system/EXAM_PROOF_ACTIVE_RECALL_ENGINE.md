## 🏛️ SECTION 1: The "One-Breath" Exam Definitions
*Use these concise, punchy definitions for 2-mark and 5-mark short-answer questions on the midterm/final exam.*

### 1. Epistemicide
* **What is it?** The systematic extermination, destruction, and delegitimization of non-Western knowledge systems, languages, and ways of knowing by a dominant colonial power.
* **Who coined it?** Boaventura de Sousa Santos (expanded by Ramón Grosfoguel).
* **The Exam Keyword**: **"Cognitive counterpart to genocide."** Physical genocide kills the body; epistemicide kills the mind, the cosmology, and the legal framework.
* **Why it matters**: A conqueror cannot keep stolen land unless they first destroy the authority of the original people's laws and knowledge.

### 2. The Cartesian Shift: *Ego Conquiro* $\rightarrow$ *Ego Cogito*
* **The Formula**:
  $$\text{Ego Conquiro ("I conquer, therefore I am")} \longrightarrow \text{Ego Cogito ("I think, therefore I am")}$$
* **What it means**: René Descartes claimed that a solitary European thinker sitting in an armchair could produce pure, objective, universal truth ("I think, therefore I am"). 
* **Grosfoguel's Critique**: Descartes hid the fact that his "rationality" was backed by 150 years of imperial conquests, slavery, and colonial violence (*ego conquiro*). It created the **"Point-Zero God-Eye illusion"**—pretending Western European male thought is universal and neutral, while everyone else is "biased" or "emotional."

### 3. Settler Colonialism & The "Logic of Elimination"
* **The Key Thinker**: Patrick Wolfe (2006).
* **The Golden Exam Line**: *"Settler colonialism is a structure, not an event."*
* **Difference from Extractive Colonialism**:
  * *Extractive colonialism* (e.g., British in India): Wants cheap labor and raw resources (rubber, spices). They leave when done.
  * *Settler colonialism* (e.g., Canada, USA, Australia): Wants the **land itself**. They come to stay forever.
* **Why Epistemicide is Required**: Because settler colonialism wants permanent possession of the land, it must eliminate Indigenous sovereignty. To eliminate Indigenous sovereignty over land, it must eliminate their legal traditions (*epistemicide*).

## 📜 SECTION 3: The Memorial to Sir Wilfrid Laurier (Kamloops, August 25, 1910)
*This is the core primary document Dr. Svenson tests. Know the actors, the Indigenous terms, and the 4 mechanisms of epistemicide.*

### 1. The Key Figures
* **The Nations**: Secwépemc (Shuswap), Nlaka'pamux (Couteau), Syilx (Okanagan).
* **The Chiefs**: Chief Petit Louis (Secwépemc), Chief John Tetlenitsa (Nlaka'pamux), Chief John Chilahitsa (Syilx).
* **The Scribe/Ally**: James Teit (Scottish ethnologist who typed their spoken words into English).
* **The Recipient**: Prime Minister Sir Wilfrid Laurier (on a pre-election campaign tour in Kamloops).

### 2. The Three Indigenous Constitutional Terms
* ***yiri7 re stsq'ey's-kucw***: The unwritten, ancient laws and customs governing Indigenous land ownership and jurisdiction.
* ***Stspet'kwle***: The ancient oral histories that carry the laws and treaties across generations.
* ***Sk'el'ep***: Coyote, the transformer/teacher who laid out the traditional boundaries, laws, and ethics 5,000+ years ago.

### 3. The 4 Canadian Mechanisms of Epistemicide (How it was Done)
1. **Oral Law Dismissed as "Myth"**:
   * Canadian courts and officials refused to acknowledge *yiri7 re stsq'ey's-kucw* as valid jurisprudence because it was oral rather than written on parchment. They labeled sophisticated constitutional law as "primitive folk stories."
2. **Inversion of the Host-Guest Relationship**:
   * When European fur traders (Hudson's Bay Co.) arrived, the Chiefs welcomed them as **guests** under Indigenous laws of hospitality and reciprocity.
   * As settlers outnumbered Indigenous people, they inverted the relationship: the "guest" claimed to be the "master," declaring Crown sovereignty and locking the sovereign hosts onto tiny reserves without treaty or consent.
   * *Direct Quote to Remember*: Settlers claimed they have *"authority over us,"* while they *"broken down our old laws and customs"* and *"laugh at our chiefs and brush them aside."*
3. **Legal Muzzling (Section 141 of the Indian Act - 1927)**:
   * When Indigenous nations organized politically to hire British lawyers to take their land claims to the Privy Council in London, the Canadian Parliament amended the Indian Act to make it a **crime to hire a lawyer or raise funds for land claims**.
   * *Exam significance*: The state literally outlawed Indigenous legal defense.
4. **Residential Schools (TRC / Justice Murray Sinclair)**:
   * Mandatory child abduction to destroy language, ceremonies, and familial knowledge transmission.
   * *Sinclair's Quote*: Education was used as the weapon of oppression, so education must be the tool for reconciliation.

## 🔬 SECTION 4: Qualitative vs. Quantitative Research (Van den Hoonaard Ch. 1)
*Guaranteed 10-mark exam comparison table.*

| Dimension | Quantitative Research (Positivist) | Qualitative Research (Interpretive / Critical) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Logic** | **Deductive**: Theory $\rightarrow$ Hypothesis $\rightarrow$ Data $\rightarrow$ Confirmation. | **Inductive**: Observations $\rightarrow$ Patterns $\rightarrow$ Emergent Concepts $\rightarrow$ Theory. |
| **View of Reality (Ontology)** | Single, objective, measurable reality external to the observer. | Multiple, socially constructed realities created through human interaction and symbols. |
| **Role of Participant** | "Subject" answering pre-set, closed-ended survey boxes. | Active co-creator who **defines what is meaningful in their own experience**. |
| **Researcher Role** | Strives for detached, neutral objectivity ("God-eye" view). | **Reflexive**: Openly examines their own background, biases, and power dynamics. |
| **Key Example to Quote** | Counting how many times a widow sees her kids (assumes more = happier). | Interviewing the widow: discovering that once-a-month visits give peace and autonomy, while daily visits cause tension. |

## 🎯 SECTION 5: High-Yield Practice Exam Questions & Model Answers

### Question 1 (Short Answer - 5 Marks):
**"What is the difference between extractive colonialism and settler colonialism, and why does settler colonialism rely on epistemicide?"**

> **Model Answer (Handwritten bullet format for exam)**:
> * Extractive colonialism focuses on extracting raw resources and exploiting local labor (e.g., rubber, gold), without the intent of permanently replacing the native population.
> * Settler colonialism, as theorized by Patrick Wolfe, is an ongoing societal structure driven by a "logic of elimination" that seeks permanent possession of the land itself.
> * Because the settler state intends to stay permanently, it must eliminate Indigenous claims to territorial sovereignty.
> * Physical land theft requires moral and legal justification; therefore, the settler state uses epistemicide to delegitimize Indigenous legal traditions, spiritual covenants, and governance systems (replacing them with the fiction of *terra nullius* and Crown authority). You cannot seize territory without first destroying the legal epistemology that governs it.

### Question 2 (Long Essay - 15 Marks):
**"Explain how Ramón Grosfoguel connects René Descartes' *ego cogito* to European imperialism, and list the four epistemicides that built modern Western knowledge."**

> **Model Answer Structure**:
> 1. **Deconstructing Descartes**:
>    * Descartes established modern Western philosophy on *"Cogito, ergo sum"* ("I think, therefore I am"), claiming a disembodied, universal rational thinker.
>    * Grosfoguel reveals that *ego cogito* was preceded by 150 years of *ego conquiro* ("I conquer, therefore I am"). The European male could only imagine himself as the universal thinker because European imperial armies were conquering the globe.
>    * This established the "Point Zero" God-eye illusion: European knowledge claimed to be neutral, objective, and universal, while all non-Western knowledge was demoted to localized "superstition" or "ethnography."
> 2. **The Four Epistemicides (A-A-A-W)**:
>    * **1492 Al-Andalus**: Expulsion of Muslims/Jews; burning of Islamic libraries during the Reconquista.
>    * **16th Century Americas**: Genocide of Indigenous populations; burning of Mayan/Aztec codices and Andean quipus.
>    * **Transatlantic Slave Trade**: Kidnapping Africans; stripping ancestral languages, metallurgy, and cosmologies into physical chattel.
>    * **European Witch Hunts**: Burning hundreds of thousands of female herbalists and midwives; destroying autonomous women's ecological knowledge to install patriarchal science.
