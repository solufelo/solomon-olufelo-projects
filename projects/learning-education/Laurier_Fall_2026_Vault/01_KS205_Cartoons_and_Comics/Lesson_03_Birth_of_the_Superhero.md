## 1. Scott McCloud: The 6 Steps of Any Art (Chapter 7)
Art creation and perception progress across 6 nested layers:
1. **Idea / Purpose:** The core philosophy, impulse, emotion, and content of the work.
2. **Form:** The physical medium chosen (comic book, oil painting, song, cinema).
3. **Idiom:** The school of art or genre (superhero, noir, cubism, shonen manga).
4. **Structure:** How the work is architected (pacing, layout, panel progression).
5. **Craft:** Technical mastery (drawing skill, inking, lettering, anatomy).
6. **Surface:** Cosmetic finish (color, sheen, rendering) — first seen by readers, last built by creators!

## 2. Scott McCloud: A Word About Color (Chapter 8)
* **4-Color Printing (CMYK):** Early comic books relied on cheap newsprint and four basic inks: Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, blacK.
* **Iconic Superheroes:** Subtlety was impossible, so creators used **bold, flat primary colors** (Superman's blue & red; Wonder Woman's red & gold).
* **Recognition:** Primary colors transformed characters into vivid, instantly identifiable icons across crowded newsstands.

## 3. The Golden Age Trinity (1938–1942)
* **Action Comics #1 (1938) — Superman:** Created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster. Born out of Great Depression, immigrant/Moses allegory, champion of the oppressed.
* **Detective Comics #27 (1939) — Batman:** Created by Bob Kane and Bill Finger. Wealthy pulp vigilante responding to trauma.
* **Sensation Comics #1 (1942) — Wonder Woman:** Created by Dr. William Moulton Marston. Feminist psychological allegory of peace, love, and truth.

## 4. Matthew J. Smith: Superhero Genre Conventions
* Extraordinary powers or superior martial ability.
* Distinctive, iconic costume / iconography.
* Secret identity protecting loved ones.
* Origin story establishing moral motivation.
* Unselfish prosocial mission to defend the public good.
