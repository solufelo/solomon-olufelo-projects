## 2. Van den Hoonaard Chapter 2: Asking Questions & Identifying Goals

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 POSITIVISM (COMTE)       VS.     INTERPRETIVISM (WEBER/MEAD)│
├──────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┤
│ Auguste Comte (1798–1857)                │ Max Weber (1864–1920)            │
│ Realist ontology: One objective world    │ Relativist / Interpretive world  │
│ Goal: Uncover universal causal laws      │ Goal: *Verstehen* (Understanding)│
│ Method: Detached statistical measurement │ Method: Empathic engagement      │
│ Researcher is neutral & objective        │ Researcher is reflexive observer │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Foundational Theoretical Pillars:
1. **Max Weber’s *Verstehen***:
   - German for *"sympathetic / interpretive understanding."*
   - Sociologists cannot study humans the way chemists study molecules. Molecules have no internal motives; humans assign meanings to their actions. The researcher must see the world from the participant’s subjective viewpoint.
2. **Definition of the Situation (W.I. Thomas & Symbolic Interactionism)**:
   - *"If human beings define situations as real, they are real in their consequences."*
   - Human behavior is governed not by the objective physical environment, but by subjective interpretations of that environment.
3. **Symbolic Interactionism (Herbert Blumer & George Herbert Mead)**:
   - Humans act toward things based on the meanings those things have for them.
   - Meanings arise out of social interaction.
   - Meanings are handled and modified through an ongoing interpretive process.
4. **Harold Garfinkel’s Breaching Experiments**:
   - Deliberately violating unwritten everyday social norms to reveal the fragile, socially constructed consensus that holds society together (e.g., Deborah Landry’s University of Ottawa flash mob breaching study).

## 3. Indigenous Treaties & Epistemic Relationality

Dr. Svenson introduces two foundational Indigenous diplomatic frameworks that establish an alternative to European imperial extractivism:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     INDIGENOUS DIPLOMATIC TREATIES                          │

├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Anishinaabe & Haudenosaunee treaty │ • 1613 treaty: Haudenosaunee & Dutch │

│   and land resources for all.        │   and Mutual Respect.                │
│ • The "One Spoon": All eat together; │ • 2 Purple Rows: Haudenosaunee canoe │
│   NO KNIVES (no violence); take only │   and European ship traveling in     │
│   what you need; leave food for next;│   parallel down the river of life;   │
│   keep the dish clean.               │   NEITHER steers the other's vessel! │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Methodological Application to Qualitative Research:
* **The Dish with One Spoon**: Research is a shared ecosystem. Researchers cannot extract data greedily; they must take only what is ethically needed, leave benefits behind for the community, and protect the integrity ("cleanliness") of the field.
* **The Two Row Wampum (*Kaswentha*)**: A model for decolonizing research ethics. Non-Indigenous researchers must travel in parallel with Indigenous communities, respecting autonomous sovereignty and self-determination without attempting to steer or assimilate Indigenous knowledge systems into Western paradigms.

## 4. Van den Hoonaard Chapter 3: Strategies for Designing Research

### A. C. Wright Mills (1959): The Sociological Imagination
* The intellectual capacity to see the intersection between **personal biography** and **historical social structures**.
* Distinguishes between:
  - **Private Troubles**: An individual’s personal hardships occurring within their immediate character or local relationships (e.g., one person being unemployed).
  - **Public Issues**: Systemic contradictions and crises transcending local environments and rooted in broader institutional structures (e.g., widespread unemployment caused by industrial collapse).

### B. Howard Becker (1967): Undermining the "Hierarchy of Credibility"
* **Definition**: The deeply ingrained social bias that individuals with the highest social prestige (judges, CEOs, police chiefs, university professors) possess the most objective, credible, and truthful account of reality, while marginalized populations (prisoners, street youth, sex workers, Indigenous peoples) are inherently untrustworthy or biased.
* **The Qualitative Mission**: Qualitative sociology systematically **undermines the hierarchy of credibility** by centering the voices of those at the bottom of the pyramid.
* **The Danger of "Parachute / Helicopter Research"**: Outside researchers flying into marginalized or Indigenous communities, extracting interview data for academic publication, and departing without community consultation or reciprocal benefit.

### C. Participatory Action Research (PAR)
* **Core Characteristics**:
  1. Full collaboration between researchers and participants in all phases (from question design to dissemination).
  2. Co-construction of knowledge (participants are co-researchers, not passive subjects).
  3. Action-oriented: Explicitly aims to influence public policy, improve living conditions, and empower the marginalized group.

## 6. High-Yield Lab Flashcards (10-Minute Pre-Sleep Lock)

> **Q1: What does "A Dish with One Spoon" signify in Indigenous diplomacy and research ethics?**  
> **A**: An ancient treaty covenant between Anishinaabe and Haudenosaunee nations establishing shared stewardship of land resources. The dish represents shared territory; the one spoon means eating together peacefully with no knives (no violence), taking only what is needed, leaving enough for others, and keeping the dish clean.

> **Q2: What is the visual and diplomatic meaning of the Two Row Wampum (*Kaswentha*)?**  
> **A**: A 1613 treaty between the Haudenosaunee and Dutch. Three white bead rows symbolize peace, friendship, and mutual respect. Two parallel purple bead rows represent the Haudenosaunee canoe and European ship sailing side-by-side down the river of life—neither steering the other's vessel, embodying mutual non-interference and distinct sovereignty.

> **Q3: What is Howard Becker's "Hierarchy of Credibility" (1967)?**  
> **A**: The institutional presumption that high-status individuals (police chiefs, administrators) possess the truth, while marginalized groups are biased or unreliable. Qualitative research undermines this hierarchy by centering the voices of those at the bottom.

> **Q4: Define Max Weber’s *Verstehen*.**  
> **A**: "Sympathetic / interpretive understanding"—the qualitative mandate to understand human social action from the subjective perspective of the actors themselves.

> **Q5: What paradigm guides Bundy’s (2019) study on African Nova Scotian women and police?**  
> **A**: The **Critical Paradigm**, evidenced by its critique of institutionalized racial power imbalances and its emancipatory goal of social justice and police accountability.
