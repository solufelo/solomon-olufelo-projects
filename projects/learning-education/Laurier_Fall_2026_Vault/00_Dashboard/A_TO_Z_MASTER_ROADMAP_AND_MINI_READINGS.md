## 🔬 COURSE 1: SY 281 A (Qualitative Research Methods)

### Mini-Reading 1.1: Deborah K. van den Hoonaard — *Qualitative Research in Action* (Ch 1–4)
* **Core Argument:** Social life cannot be measured like physical gravity. Humans act based on the *meanings* they assign to things. Qualitative research is **inductive** (building theory from observation upward), whereas quantitative research is **deductive** (testing fixed hypotheses top-down).
* **Key Concept — Generic Social Processes (GSPs):** Human interaction follows recurring patterns across completely different settings. Examples include:
  1. *Acquiring an identity* (how someone becomes a "gamer," an "artist," or a "deviant").
  2. *Managing stigma* (how people hide or negotiate social judgment).
  3. *Defining situations* (how people agree on what is happening in a room).
* **Key Concept — Sensitizing Concepts:** Broad ideas that provide a general sense of reference and guidance in approaching empirical instances (contrasted with fixed, rigid quantitative definitions).
* **Exam Application:** Whenever asked how to design a qualitative study, state: *"I would use an inductive approach to observe Generic Social Processes (GSPs) using sensitizing concepts, allowing grounded theory to emerge from the participants' lived experiences."*

### Mini-Reading 1.2: Ramón Grosfoguel (2013) — *The Structure of Knowledge in Westernized Universities*
* **Core Argument:** Western universities claim to produce "universal, detached, objective truth," but this is an imperial illusion (**epistemic racism/sexism**). European men established themselves as the sole arbiters of knowledge by physically and intellectually destroying competing worldviews.
* **The 4 Epistemicides of the Long 16th Century:**
  1. **1492 (Spain):** Conquest of Al-Andalus; expulsion of Moors and Jews; burning of massive Arabic/Hebrew scientific libraries.
  2. **1492–1600s (Americas):** Genocide of Indigenous peoples of Abya Yala; burning of sacred Mayan, Aztec, and Andean codices.
  3. **1500s–1800s (Africa):** Transatlantic slave trade; commodifying Africans into chattel; eradicating ancestral cosmologies and metallurgical knowledge.
  4. **1500s–1600s (Europe):** Burning hundreds of thousands of female healers and midwives as "witches" to establish patriarchal, capitalist medical science.
* **The Cartesian Link:** René Descartes' famous principle *Ego cogito* ("I think, therefore I am") was only possible because it was preceded by 150 years of *Ego conquiro* ("I conquer, therefore I am"). Imperial conquest provided the arrogance to claim a "God's eye view."
* **Exam Application:** Connects directly to decolonizing methodologies and why qualitative researchers must practice **reflexivity** (examining their own social location and power privileges).

### Mini-Reading 1.3: Chiefs of Shuswap, Okanagan & Couteau Tribes (1910) — *Memorial to Sir Wilfrid Laurier*
* **Core Argument:** Sovereign Indigenous nations welcomed European settlers as guests and equal allies under sacred oral treaties. The Canadian settler state committed epistemicide and theft by unilaterally declaring sovereignty, passing laws banning lawyers for land claims (Section 141 of Indian Act), and tearing down Indigenous legal systems.
* **Key Concept — Indigenous Oral Jurisprudence (*yiri7 re stsq'ey's-kucw*):** Legal titles and boundary treaties preserved through oral traditions (*Stspet'kwle*) and Coyote (*Sk'el'ep*) stories, which the British colonial state dismissed as "primitive myths."
* **Exam Application:** The primary empirical historical proof of Canadian epistemicide in qualitative research.

### Mini-Reading 1.4: Merrigan et al. (2012) — *Paradigms of Knowing*
* **The 3 Inquiry Paradigms:**
  1. **Knowing by Discovery (Quantitative):** Objective reality; detached observer; searches for universal causal laws and statistical correlations.
  2. **Knowing by Interpretation (Qualitative / Dr. Svenson):** Multiple constructed realities; knower and reality are inseparable; seeks rich, deep subjective understanding (*Verstehen*) of human action.
  3. **Knowing by Criticism (Critical):** Reality shaped by historical power structures and ideological dominance; goal is exposing oppression and creating social justice.
* **Alfred Schutz’s Lifeworld (*Lebenswelt*):** The shared, inter-subjective world of everyday common-sense reality that people take for granted.
* **Exam Application:** Dr. Svenson's exam questions will ask you to identify which paradigm a researcher is using. If they want statistical numbers $\rightarrow$ Discovery. If they want participants' personal feelings/meanings $\rightarrow$ Interpretation. If they want to expose racism/classism $\rightarrow$ Criticism.

### Mini-Reading 1.5: Canadian Research Ethics — *TCPS 2 Framework*
* **The 3 Core Ethical Pillars:**
  1. **Respect for Persons:** Free, informed, ongoing consent; absolute protection of vulnerable populations.
  2. **Concern for Welfare:** Protecting participants from physical, mental, social, or legal harm; ensuring strict confidentiality and pseudonymity.
  3. **Justice:** Treating people fairly and equitably; ensuring no single group carries all research burdens while another takes all benefits.
* **REB (Research Ethics Board):** The institutional gatekeeper that must approve any university research involving living human participants.

## 🍜 COURSE 2: FS 209C A (Food and Identity in Film)

### Mini-Reading 2.1: Roland Barthes & Ferdinand de Saussure — *Culinary Semiotics*
* **Core Argument:** Food is never just calories; food is a semiotic language system.
  * **Signifier:** The physical, material object (e.g., a steaming bowl of noodles, a raw egg yolk, a plate of soul food greens).
  * **Signified:** The mental, cultural, and ideological concept attached to it (e.g., poverty, maternal love, sexual desire, diaspora, heritage).
* **Myth (Barthes):** When culture turns a social construction into something that seems "natural" and unquestioned (e.g., associating French wine with "sophistication" or fast food with "laziness").

### Mini-Reading 2.2: Alfred Crosby (1972) — *The Columbian Exchange*
* **Core Argument:** Post-1492 global history is defined by the massive ecological transfer of plants, animals, culture, and disease between the Old World (Afro-Eurasia) and the New World (Americas).
* **The Food Revolution:** Before 1492:
  * Italy had NO tomatoes.
  * Ireland and Russia had NO potatoes.
  * Thailand, India, and China had NO chili peppers.
  * Switzerland had NO chocolate.
  * All of these were Indigenous American domestications! Globalization created the modern culinary world through colonial trade and imperial violence.

### Mini-Reading 2.3: Sidney Cheung — *Hybridity and Authenticity in Foodways*
* **Core Argument:** "Authenticity" in food is a social myth. All national cuisines are constantly evolving hybrids shaped by trade, migration, and adaptation. Claiming a dish is "100% authentic" is a political act of cultural gatekeeping. Migrant communities adapt traditional dishes to local ingredients to survive and maintain identity.

### Mini-Reading 2.4: Frederick Douglass Opie (2008) — *Hog and Hominy: The Chitlin Circuit*
* **Core Argument:** Soul food originated in the brutal survival strategies of enslaved Africans in the American South, who took the discarded, least-desirable scraps of livestock (chitterlings/pig intestines, pig feet, ham hocks, collard greens, cornmeal) and transformed them through West African culinary genius into celebrated cuisine.
* **The Meaning of "Soul":** During the 1960s Civil Rights and Black Power movements, "soul" became an expression of Black resilience, cultural pride, and solidarity against white supremacy.

### The 3 Core Films Breakdown:
1. ***Tampopo* (Juzo Itami, 1985):** The Japanese "Ramen Western." Celebrates the **Shokunin** spirit (craft devotion). Uses satirical vignettes to show food as sex (gangster raw egg yolk), food as class defiance (junior salaryman ordering French delicacies), and food as maternal duty (dying mother cooking fried rice).
2. ***Minari* (Lee Isaac Chung, 2020):** Immigrant resilience. The *minari* plant (water parsley) functions on 3 levels: literal plant by the muddy creek; cultural connection between Korean grandmother and American grandson; philosophical proof that resilience thrives where water flows freely, outlasting Jacob's burnt capitalist commercial farm.
3. ***Moonlight* (Barry Jenkins, 2016):** Kevin prepares a hot, tender Cuban meal (pork, rice, black beans, fried plantains) for Chiron in a late-night diner. Food becomes an intimate sanctuary of vulnerability, healing from childhood trauma, and queer Black tenderness.

## 🎨 COURSE 3: KS 205 VA (Cartoons and Comics)

### Mini-Reading 3.1: Scott McCloud — *Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art* (The Complete 8-Chapter Canon)
* **Ch 1 (Definition):** *"Juxtaposed pictorial and other images in deliberate sequence, intended to convey information and/or produce an aesthetic response."* Will Eisner calls it *"Sequential Art."*
* **Ch 2 (Vocabulary & Icons):** Cartoons work through **"Amplification through Simplification."** When detail is stripped away, the reader projects their own identity into the universal cartoon face (The Masking Effect).
* **Ch 3 (The Gutter & The 6 Closure Transitions):**
  * **Closure:** Mentally connecting incomplete parts to perceive the whole.
  * **The Gutter:** The blank space between panels where the reader's imagination creates the action.
  * **6 Transitions:** 1. Moment-to-moment | 2. Action-to-action (most common in West) | 3. Subject-to-subject | 4. Scene-to-scene | 5. Aspect-to-aspect (dominant in Japanese manga) | 6. Non-sequitur.
* **Ch 4 (Time Frames):** Space equals time. Panel size and width dictate duration. Borderless bleeds create a feeling of timelessness.
* **Ch 5 (Living in Line):** **Synaesthesia** (visual lines provoking sound, temperature, and emotion). Jagged thick lines = anger/danger; soft curves = intimacy/calm.
* **Ch 6 (Show and Tell):** Word/image dynamics. **Interdependent** is the highest form (words + pictures convey what neither could alone).
* **Ch 7 (The 6 Steps of Art):** Idea $\rightarrow$ Form $\rightarrow$ Idiom $\rightarrow$ Structure $\rightarrow$ Craft $\rightarrow$ Surface.
* **Ch 8 (Color):** 4-color CMYK process on cheap pulp gave birth to flat, bold, primary-colored superheroes (iconic recognition).

### Mini-Reading 3.2: Mark Newgarden & Paul Karasik — *How to Read Nancy*
* **Core Argument:** A three-panel comic strip like Ernie Bushmiller's *Nancy* looks simplistic to a casual reader, but is actually an astonishing feat of mechanical engineering. Every line, shape, horizon line, and eye-direction is calculated with surgical precision to deliver a gag in less than 3 seconds.
* **Exam Value:** This is the exact template you will use for Saturday's Newspaper Strip Analysis!

### Mini-Reading 3.3: Matthew J. Smith — *Superhero Comics*
* **Core Conventions of the Superhero Genre:**
  1. Superhuman abilities or elite specialized technology.
  2. Distinctive, iconic costume with trademark logo.
  3. Secret dual-identity protecting loved ones.
  4. Tragic/transformative origin story creating moral imperative.
  5. Unselfish, prosocial mission dedicated to public justice.
* **The Golden Age Trinity:** *Action Comics #1* (Superman, 1938 — immigrant New Deal champion); *Detective Comics #27* (Batman, 1939 — trauma-driven urban vigilante); *Sensation Comics #1* (Wonder Woman, 1942 — feminist psychological peace allegory).
