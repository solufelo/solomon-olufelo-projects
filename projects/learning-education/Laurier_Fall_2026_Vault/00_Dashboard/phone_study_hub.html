1. The Cartesian Link (Grosfoguel 2013)

Descartes' 1637 formula Ego Cogito, Ergo Sum ("I think, therefore I am") did not come from nowhere. It was preceded by 150 years of imperialism:

Ego Conquiro ("I conquer") ➔ Ego Extermino ("I exterminate") ➔ Ego Cogito ("I think")

The God-Eye / Point-Zero View: The Western male claims to produce detached, universal truth from nowhere. Decolonial research rejects this illusion, insisting on situated, embodied, reflexive knowledge.

The 4 Epistemicides of the 16th Century:
  • Al-Andalus (1492): Muslims/Jews expelled; 1M+ scientific books burned; limpieza de sangre.
  • Americas (1492+): Indigenous genocide; Valladolid debate (Sepúlveda vs. Las Casas).
  • Transatlantic Slavery: Africans classified as soulless chattel; epistemic erasure.
  • Witch Hunts: Female herbalists/midwives burned; medicine monopolized by patriarchal universities.
2. 1910 Memorial to Sir Wilfrid Laurier

Presented at Kamloops on Aug 25, 1910 by Chief Petit Louis (Secwépemc), Chief John Tetlenitsa (Nlaka'pamux), and Chief John Chilahitsa (Syilx), assisted by secretary James Teit.

  • Host-Guest Covenant: Indigenous nations are perpetual hosts. Under ancient law (yirí7 re stsq'ey's-kucw / Stspetékwle), outsiders are welcomed as guests. Fur traders respected this; post-1858 ranchers and Joseph Trutch violated it by stealing land and water.
  • Patrick Wolfe's Logic of Elimination: Settler colonialism is a structure, not an event; it seeks to eliminate Indigenous presence to claim land.
  • Section 141 (Indian Act, 1927): Outlawed hiring lawyers or raising money for Indigenous land claims.

Week 1: Foundations of Qualitative Inquiry

Qualitative research uses inductive logic (bottom-up generation of meaning from empirical observation) rather than deductive hypothesis testing.

Sensitizing Concepts (Herbert Blumer)
Unlike quantitative definitive concepts (rigid metrics like "income < $25,000"), sensitizing concepts provide a general sense of reference and orientation (e.g., "stigma", "identity management", "liminality").
Characteristics of Qualitative Methods
Rigorous, processual, theoretically based, contextual, recognizes complexity, and experiential. Emergent design means the study evolves dynamically as the researcher learns in the field.
Week 2: Epistemicide & Western Universities

How the Western university canon was formed through imperial violence rather than neutral intellectual superiority.

The Canon of 5 Nations
Grosfoguel highlights that 95%+ of university social theory is written by men from only five countries: France, Germany, Great Britain, Italy, and the USA. Their local observations are universalized, while Global South knowledge is segregated into "Area Studies."
Transmodernity (Enrique Dussel)
Rather than European universalism (one worldview for all) or postmodern relativism (nihilism), Dussel proposes Pluriversality: a world where many worlds fit through horizontal dialogue among diverse epistemic traditions.
Week 3: Paradigms & Radical Hermeneutics
Case Study: "Poor Tetsuo" Lifeworld Clash
Tetsuo, a Japanese exchange student, gets caught between rural townspeople (folk lifeworld where evil/demonic forces are real) and urban visitors (modern secular lifeworld of irony/tourism). Thomas Kuhn: Paradigms & Revolutions
A paradigm is a shared framework of theories, standards, and methods. Normal science operates within it until anomalies trigger a crisis, leading to a revolutionary paradigm shift.

Ernie Bushmiller’s *Nancy* (Newgarden & Karasik)

"Ernie Bushmiller had the hand of an architect, the mind of a silent film comedian, and the soul of an accountant."

  • Spotting the Blacks: Connect-the-dots chiaroscuro pulls the eye across panels like an undulating wave.
  • Groundline Swell: The upward slope of the ground in the punchline panel elevates Nancy, stops Sluggo’s momentum, and frames the seltzer bottle.
  • Dialogue Counterpoint: Repeating Sluggo's line three times minimizes reading load, focusing attention on his shifting physical posture.
Scott McCloud: *Understanding Comics* (Ch 1–6)
  • Definition: "Juxtaposed pictorial and other images in deliberate sequence, intended to convey information and/or to produce an aesthetic response."
  • Masking Effect: Simple cartoon icon for protagonist (universal identification) + realistic background (grounded world).
  • 6 Closure Transitions: (1) Moment-to-moment, (2) Action-to-action, (3) Subject-to-subject, (4) Scene-to-scene, (5) Aspect-to-aspect, (6) Non-sequitur.
  • Interdependent Combination: Words and pictures working together to communicate an idea that neither could convey alone.
