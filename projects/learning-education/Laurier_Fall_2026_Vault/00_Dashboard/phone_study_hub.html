🌿 Week 4 Treaties: One Spoon & Two Row Wampum

Dr. Svenson contrasts colonial extractivism with two historic Indigenous diplomatic frameworks:

1. A Dish with One Spoon (Anishinaabe & Haudenosaunee covenant):
  • The Dish: Shared territory and hunting grounds stewarded for all inhabitants.
  • The Spoon: Eating together peacefully. No sharp knives allowed (no warfare); take only what you need; leave food for others; keep the dish clean.
  • Research Parallel: Researchers must take only necessary data, protect the field, and leave benefits for the community.
2. The Two Row Wampum (*Kaswentha*, 1613) (Haudenosaunee & Dutch):
  • 3 White Rows: Peace, Friendship, and Mutual Respect.
  • 2 Purple Rows: The Haudenosaunee canoe and European ship sailing in parallel down the river of life.
  • Core Law: Neither vessel steers the other! Mutual non-interference and distinct, equal sovereignty.
🏛️ Howard Becker: "Hierarchy of Credibility"

Howard Becker (1967): The bias that high-status individuals (police chiefs, administrators) possess the truth, while marginalized groups are untrustworthy.

Qualitative Sociology Mission: Systematically undermine the hierarchy of credibility by centering the voices of those at the bottom of the pyramid.

Danger of "Parachute / Helicopter Research": Outside researchers extracting interview data from Indigenous or racialized groups and leaving without reciprocity.

Participatory Action Research (PAR): Full collaboration in all stages; co-construction of knowledge; aimed at policy change and community empowerment.
