## 1. Culinary Semiotics (Barthes & Saussure)
* **Signifier:** The physical food item (e.g., bowl of ramen, bowl of white rice, banchan).
* **Signified:** The cultural, social, and emotional meaning (class, diaspora, love, ritual).
* Food is never merely biological fuel; it is a complex visual and narrative grammar.

---

## 2. Tampopo (Juzo Itami, 1985) — 5 Key Vignettes
1. **The Shokunin Master:** Elderly ramen master teaching the precise aesthetic ritual of greeting pork and noodles.
2. **Gangster & Mistress (Erotic Gastronomy):** Passing live raw egg yolk mouth-to-mouth; food as sensual desire and mortality.
3. **Junior Executive French Restaurant:** Politeness hierarchy shattered when the junior orders quenelles and vintage wine while senior bosses fumble.
4. **Spaghetti Etiquette Lesson:** Western teacher tries to teach silent pasta eating; disrupted by a foreigner slurping loudly.
5. **Dying Mother's Final Meal:** Mother collapses and dies immediately after cooking one last fried rice for her family.

---

## 3. Minari (Lee Isaac Chung, 2020) — The 3-Level Metaphor
1. **Level 1 (Literal):** Resilient East Asian water parsley growing wildly near the dirty creek.
2. **Level 2 (Cultural/Intergenerational):** Grandmother Soon-ja gifting ancestral seeds to connect Korean roots with Arkansas soil.
3. **Level 3 (Existential/Philosophical):** Unlike Jacob's capitalist commercial farm (which burns to ashes), *minari* flourishes where water flows freely.
